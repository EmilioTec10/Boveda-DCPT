## 📚 Idea/Concept 

IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS represent three layers of cloud service models that differ primarily in how responsibility and control are divided between the cloud provider and the customer. Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) offers virtualized computing resources such as virtual machines, storage, and networking while granting the customer full control over the operating system, middleware, and applications. This model provides flexibility but places operational burden on the user. Platform as a Service (PaaS) abstracts away the operating system, runtime, and middleware, enabling developers to deploy applications without managing the underlying environment. Its agility comes from this complete runtime abstraction, though it introduces compromises in control and may increase vendor lock-in. Finally, Software as a Service (SaaS) delivers fully managed applications accessible over the internet, typically built on multitenant architectures that maximize scalability and cost efficiency. Each model offers a different balance between control, operational overhead, and convenience, shaping how organizations build and deliver software in the cloud.
## 📌 Key points (Optional)
- IaaS provides full OS-level control, whereas PaaS and SaaS trade control for simplicity, automation, and reduced operational burden.
- PaaS abstracts the runtime and middleware to accelerate development, while SaaS commonly uses multitenant architectures to offer scalable, cost-efficient applications.
