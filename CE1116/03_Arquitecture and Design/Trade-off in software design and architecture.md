## 📚 Idea/Concept 

A trade-off in software design and architecture is an informed, risk-oriented compromise decision that balances competing quality attributes—such as performance, availability, scalability, or maintainability—by considering the technical, economic, and risk costs of each alternative. These decisions, which are generally irreversible, require in-depth technical knowledge to assess impacts and must be justified and documented with an architectural rationale that includes the discarded alternatives and their rationales. A well-managed trade-off seeks the most cost-effective and sustainable option, mitigating critical risks through prototypes or proofs of concept (PoC) and avoiding the accumulation of technical debt, which represents the long-term cost of suboptimal architectural decisions.
## 📌 Key points (Optional)
- It is an **informed, risk-oriented compromise** that balances **competing quality attributes** like performance, availability, and scalability based on technical and economic impact.  
- It requires **deep technical evaluation and clear documentation** of the rationale, including discarded alternatives and their justifications.
